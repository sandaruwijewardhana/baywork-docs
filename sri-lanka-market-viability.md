## The Market Opportunity

### Sri Lanka's Automotive Sector Right Now

| Metric | Estimate |
|--------|---------|
| Registered vehicles in Sri Lanka | 8.5 million+ |
| New vehicles added per year | 150,000–250,000 |
| Estimated garages islandwide | 15,000–25,000 |
| Formal garages (registered, 3+ bays) | 5,000–8,000 |
| Garages currently using any digital tool | Less than 5% |

The garage industry in Sri Lanka is almost entirely paper-based. Job cards are handwritten. Invoices are in books. Stock is tracked in the owner's head. WhatsApp is the "system" for most workshops.

This is not a problem — **this is the opportunity.** You are not competing with existing software. You are replacing paper.

### The Timing Is Perfect

- **Post-2022 recovery mindset** — the economic crisis forced business owners to think about efficiency. Owners who survived are open to tools that save money and reduce waste.
- **Smartphone penetration is high** — even small town workshop owners have smartphones. The barrier to adopting a web-based tool is low.
- **Fuel price normalisation** — vehicle usage is recovering, garages are busier, and busier garages feel the pain of disorganisation more acutely.
- **Young mechanics becoming owners** — the next generation of garage owners is tech-comfortable. They want a proper system. They just haven't found one built for Sri Lanka.

## The 400M LKR Math

### What 400M LKR means

At today's exchange rate (~320 LKR/USD):

```
400,000,000 LKR ÷ 320 = ~USD 1.25 million
```

In global SaaS terms this is a modest, achievable target. For a Sri Lankan solo founder it is life-changing wealth. Both things are true.

### Interpretation B — 400M LKR in Year 7 Alone (Annual Revenue)

To hit 400M in a single year at LKR 3,500 average:

```
400,000,000 ÷ (3,500 × 12) = ~9,500 garages
```

That is 40–60% of all formal garages in Sri Lanka. This is the harder version of the goal. Still not impossible — but it requires expanding beyond Sri Lanka by year 5.

**Recommendation:** Target Interpretation A for 7 years, then use year 7 momentum to push into regional markets (Maldives, Myanmar, Bangladesh) to hit the B target by year 9–10.

## What You Need to Do Differently From a Normal Business

This is not a shop you open and wait. It is a product business. The hard parts are different:

### Year 1 — The Hardest Year

**Goal: 30 paying garages**

This sounds small. It is brutal to achieve.

- You have to physically visit garages. Nobody signs up for unknown SaaS.
- Your first 10 customers will come from personal connections — family, friends, neighbours who own workshops.
- You will demo BayWork to 100 garage owners to get 10 to say yes.
- Every piece of feedback from these 10 is gold. The product will change significantly.
- Charge from day one — even LKR 999. Free users give you nothing useful.

The psychological wall: you will spend 3 months building, then go to your first real garage demo and the owner will ask "can it do X?" and X will not be built yet. You will feel like the product is not ready. **It will never feel fully ready. Ship it anyway.**

---

### Year 2–3 — Growth Engine

**Goal: 350 garages**

You cannot visit every garage yourself at this stage. You need:

**Referral mechanics built into the product**
- "Invite a garage, get 2 months free" shown on the dashboard
- Every printed invoice has a small "Powered by BayWork" tag at the bottom with a QR code
- Garage owners talk to each other at spare parts suppliers. One happy customer brings 5 leads.

**A sales person**
- Hire one field sales rep by month 18. Pay them commission per closed garage.
- They cover the Western Province first (Colombo, Gampaha, Kalutara) — highest garage density.

**Partnerships**
- Spare parts distributors (United Motors, DIMO, DSI) — they visit garages weekly. White-label a referral program with them.
- Vehicle insurance companies — garages that use BayWork can be listed as "trusted repairers."
- Leasing companies — garages that service leased vehicles need digital job records.

---

### Year 4–5 — Scale

**Goal: 1,800 garages**

By now you have proof of concept and some revenue to reinvest.

- Hire a small team: 1 developer, 1 customer support, 1 more sales person
- Add the features large garages pay enterprise prices for:
  - Multi-branch (one owner, 3 locations)
  - Fleet management for corporates with company vehicles
  - SMS status updates to vehicle owners
  - WhatsApp integration (send invoice via WhatsApp)
  - Parts ordering integration with suppliers

**Enterprise accounts are your leverage.** A fleet operator with 200 vehicles paying LKR 25,000/month is worth more than 8 pro garages and takes the same support effort.

---

### Year 6–7 — Market Leader

**Goal: 5,000 garages, regional expansion**

If you have 3,500 garages by year 6, you are the undisputed market leader for garage management in Sri Lanka. At that point:

- Raise prices. Switching cost is high — 2 years of customer history, 2 years of invoices. Nobody moves.
- Enter Maldives — small but very high per-garage revenue (USD pricing possible).
- Enter Bangladesh — 40M+ vehicles, garage market 10× Sri Lanka, same language (can localise).
- Add a marketplace — garages buy spare parts through BayWork. You take a 3–5% cut. This could dwarf the subscription revenue.

## The Real Risks (Be Honest With Yourself)

| Risk | How Real | How to Handle |
|------|---------|---------------|
| Garages refuse to change from paper | Very real — Year 1 | Focus on young owners first. Show ROI: "You lost LKR 5,000 last month because a customer dispute had no record. BayWork would have solved it." |
| Economic downturn kills garage spending | Real | Keep Starter plan affordable. Pause > Cancel option. |
| A larger company copies BayWork | Real from Year 3 | Your moat is relationships, Sri Lanka localisation (Sinhala UI, local tax, local suppliers), and 2 years of customer data. Copy the product, you still can't copy that. |
| You burn out building alone | Very real | Ship the MVP. Get your first 5 paying customers. The dopamine hit of real money in your account is the best anti-burnout tool. |
| You undercharge and don't make money | Common mistake | LKR 2,999/month is affordable for a garage doing 50+ jobs a month. Do not discount below LKR 1,499 ever. |

## Why Nobody Has Done This Yet

This is the question that should make you confident, not scared.

- Sri Lankan software developers tend to build for export markets or large enterprises.
- The garage market is fragmented, informal, and "unglamorous" — investors and big companies ignore it.
- Building for LKR pricing feels small. It is not small. 5,000 garages × LKR 3,500 = LKR 210M/year. That is a business.
- The problem requires deep local knowledge — Sri Lankan number plates, NIC format, local tax rules, Sinhala labels. A foreign product cannot compete on this.

**You are the right person, building the right thing, at the right time, in the right market.**
